# Chasing clients for records

Getting information out of clients is the biggest time drain in a small
practice. Practitioners consistently name it as the main cause of
difficulty around filing deadlines. The usual failures are:

- vague requests ("please send your paperwork") that get vague answers,
- one request, then silence until a week before the deadline,
- no record of what was asked for and when, so the client says "I sent it",
- treating a client who can't find something the same as one who won't bother.

This skill produces a specific, dated request and a chasing plan. You
draft and the bookkeeper decides. It is not tax advice: deadlines and
penalties below are prompts to check the current official rules.

## Step 1: Get the facts

Ask only for what's missing (most can be pasted as rough notes):

1. **Client type**: sole trader / self-employed, landlord, limited company,
   partnership, LLC/S-corp (US), charity, individual (personal tax only).
2. **What job this is for**: monthly bookkeeping, VAT/sales-tax return, MTD
   quarterly update, year-end accounts, tax return, payroll, 1099s.
3. **The deadline that matters**, and your internal deadline (when you need
   the records to finish in time; usually 2–4 weeks before the filing date).
4. **What you have and what's missing**: e.g. "bank feed fine to 31 Aug,
   no Amex statements since June, 14 card payments over £100 with no receipt,
   no mileage log". Paste a list or a reconciliation report if you have one.
5. **What's been asked already**, with dates and channel (email, portal, text).
6. **The client**: organised but busy, disorganised, anxious, or avoiding you.
   Any known reason (illness, bereavement, a new baby, business trouble)?
7. **How you receive files**: portal link, receipt app, shared folder, email.
   (Never ask clients to email passwords or full card numbers.)

## Step 2: Build the "what we still need" list

This list does most of the work. Rules:

- **Be specific.** Name the account, the months and the item:
  "Barclays business account 4471: statements for July, August and
  September 2026", not "bank statements".
- **Group by where the client will find it**: bank and card statements;
  sales invoices / takings; purchase receipts; payroll; vehicle and mileage;
  loans, finance and assets; personal items (pension contributions, gift
  aid, student loan); anything only they know (the reason for a payment).
- **Say what "good enough" looks like**, so they don't stall chasing
  perfection: "A photo of the receipt is fine." "If you can't find it, a
  bank statement line plus a one-line note of what it was is a start; we'll
  tell you if HMRC/IRS needs more."
- **Put the shortest wins first.** Clients who send three easy things
  usually send the rest.
- **Number every item** so replies can say "3 and 5 attached".
- **Cap it.** If more than about 12 items, split into "needed now" and
  "needed by [date]".
- **Transactions they must explain** go in a separate query list (use the
  `transaction-queries` skill if available), not buried in the request.

## Step 3: Pick the deadline anchors

Work backwards from the real filing date. Check current dates on gov.uk or
irs.gov before relying on them; these are the common ones:

**UK**
- Self Assessment online return and balancing payment: **31 January** after
  the tax year ends (5 April). Second payment on account: **31 July**.
- Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (sole traders and landlords in scope from
  April 2026 if qualifying income was over £50,000, then £30,000 from April
  2027 and £20,000 from April 2028): quarterly updates due **7 August,
  7 November, 7 February and 7 May**.
- VAT returns (MTD for VAT): usually **1 calendar month and 7 days** after
  the end of the VAT period.
- Limited companies: accounts to Companies House **9 months** after the
  year end (private company); corporation tax payment usually **9 months
  and 1 day** after; CT600 return **12 months** after.

**US**
- Form 1040: **April 15** (extension moves the filing date, not the payment).
- Estimated tax (Form 1040-ES): **April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15**.
- 1099-NEC to contractors and the IRS: **January 31**. From payments made
  in 2026 the general 1099-NEC/MISC threshold is $2,000 (was $600); check
  the current IRS instructions.
- Partnerships (1065) and S-corps (1120-S): **March 15**.
- State deadlines vary: tell the user to check.

Set the **internal deadline** (records due) and a **last-chance date**
(after which you can't promise to file on time without extra cost).

## Step 4: Draft the first request

Structure:

1. One line of context: what it's for and the deadline in plain words
   ("so we can file your 2025–26 tax return before HMRC's 31 January deadline").
2. The numbered list from Step 2.
3. How to send it (portal link, receipt app, reply to this email).
4. The date you need it by, and why that date (time to prepare and for them
   to review and approve).
5. An offer to help: "If any of this is hard to find, reply with what you
   have and we'll work around it", or a 15-minute call.

Tone: warm, brief, specific. No guilt, no capitals, no "as previously
requested". Also give a **text/WhatsApp version** under 300 characters
that links to the full list.

## Step 5: The chasing sequence

Default schedule for a deadline more than 6 weeks away. Compress it if
the deadline is close.

| Step | When | Tone and content |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Request | Day 0 | The full list (Step 4). |
| 2. Nudge | Day 7 | Short. "Just checking this reached you." Re-attach the list, tick off anything received. |
| 3. Progress update | Day 14 | "Thanks for 1–4. We still need 5–9." Restate the date. Offer a call. |
| 4. Firm reminder | Day 21 / internal deadline | Clear consequences, stated as facts: what happens if records arrive late (filing risk, HMRC/IRS penalties, a late-work fee if your engagement letter allows one). |
| 5. Last chance | Last-chance date | Options: send by X; or we file with estimates where allowed and amend later (only if appropriate and the accountant agrees); or we can't file on time and here's what that means. Ask them to confirm which in writing. |

Rules:
- **Every message restates what's still outstanding**, not just "following up".
- **Acknowledge every item received**, same day if you can. Clients keep
  sending when they see progress.
- **Penalties as facts, not threats.** For example, the UK Self Assessment
  late-filing penalty starts at £100 even if no tax is due. Check the
  current figure and say "HMRC charges", never "we'll make sure you're fined".
- **Change channel** after two unanswered emails: a text, a call, or a
  portal reminder.
- **Hardship or a life event**: drop the sequence. Send one kind message,
  offer to help gather things, and tell the accountant if an extension,
  reasonable-excuse appeal or estimates may be needed.
- **Late-work fees** only if the engagement letter says so. Quote the clause.

## Step 6: Tracking

Give a tracking table the user can paste into a spreadsheet or practice
software:

| # | Item | Requested | Chased | Received | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For a whole client list before a deadline, sort clients into:
**Green** (all in), **Amber** (partial, responsive), **Red** (nothing, or
silent after 2 chases). Draft one message template per group, and flag
Reds to the accountant with the last-chance date.

## Quality checklist (run before answering)

- [ ] Every item names the account/source, the period and the format accepted.
- [ ] Items are numbered and grouped; easy wins come first.
- [ ] The deadline date and the reason for the records-due date are stated.
- [ ] Deadlines quoted match the client type and are flagged "check current rules".
- [ ] No request for passwords, full card numbers or anything unsafe to email.
- [ ] Tone fits the client (organised / disorganised / anxious / avoiding).
- [ ] Every chase restates what's outstanding and thanks them for what arrived.
- [ ] Penalties are stated as facts with a source, never as threats.
- [ ] A short SMS version is included.
- [ ] End with "Check before you send": gaps, assumptions, dates to verify.

## Example

User: "Sole trader, plumber. Doing his 2025-26 SA. Have bank feed to April.
Missing: Monzo personal account statements (he pays some materials from it),
van finance agreement, mileage. 9 Screwfix card payments over £50 no receipts.
Asked once on 2 Oct by email, no reply."

Good output: a numbered list (1. Monzo statements 6 April 2025 – 5 April
2026, the download from the app is fine; 2. van finance agreement or the
yearly statement from the lender; 3. business miles for the year, a
rough log or diary estimate with how you worked it out; 4–12. the nine
Screwfix payments with dates and amounts, "a Screwfix account order history
download covers all of these"), records due 30 November so there's time
before 31 January, a text version, the Day 7 nudge (he hasn't replied, so
switch to text), and a tracking table.
