Client won't send their records? Email templates and a chasing schedule for bookkeepers

Every practice has the same January: a handful of clients who've gone quiet, a deadline that won't move, and a stack of "just following up" emails that haven't worked. This guide gives you a request clients can actually act on, five dated chasers to copy, the penalty facts to quote (UK and US), and a free planner that works the dates back from the deadline and puts them in your calendar.

Updated 26 October 2026 · UK and US · Penalty figures checked against gov.uk and irs.gov · General information, not tax advice

In one paragraph: clients rarely ignore you on purpose. They can't find something, don't know what you mean by "paperwork", or feel embarrassed about how far behind they are. So send one numbered list of exactly what's missing (account, months, acceptable format), a records-due date with the reason, and then chase on a fixed schedule: a nudge at 7 days, a progress update at 14, a firm reminder on the records-due date with the penalties as facts, and a last-chance message offering options in writing. Thank them for every item that arrives, and switch to a text or a call after two unanswered emails.

The "what we still need" list does most of the work

At year end, start from a tailored questionnaire rather than a generic one: our year-end questionnaire guide and free builder writes one per client with the checklist and a records-due date.

A vague request gets a vague answer. A list a client can tick off gets ticked off. The rules:

  • Name the source, the period and the item. "Monzo personal account: statements 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 (the PDF download from the app is fine)".
  • Number everything, so replies can say "3 and 5 attached".
  • Easy wins first. Clients who send three quick things usually send the rest.
  • Say what good enough looks like. "A photo is fine." "If you can't find the receipt, tell us what it was and we'll tell you if that's enough."
  • Group by where they'll find it: bank and card statements, sales, purchase receipts, vehicle and mileage, loans and assets, personal items (pension contributions, Gift Aid, student loan).
  • Keep questions separate. "What was the £1,250 to J Patel on 14 March?" belongs in a short query list, not buried in the request.
  • Cap it at about 12 items. More than that, split it into "needed now" and "needed by [date]".
  • Never ask for passwords, full card numbers or ID documents by email. Point them at your portal or upload link.

Example (a self-employed plumber's 2025/26 return, one email already ignored):

1. Monzo personal account: statements 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. You pay some materials from it. The PDF from the app is fine.
2. Van finance: the agreement, or the yearly statement from the lender.
3. Business miles for the year: a rough log, or your diary with how you worked it out.
4–12. Nine Screwfix card payments over £50 with no receipt (dates and amounts below). Your Screwfix account's order history download covers all nine.

Five emails to copy, in order

Swap the bracketed parts for the client's details. Every message restates what's still outstanding, so nobody has to dig back through old emails, and none of them says "as per my previous email".

1. The request (day 0)

Subject: What we need for your [2025/26 tax return] by [30 November]

Hi [first name],

We're getting your [2025/26 tax return] ready so it's filed before HMRC's 31 January deadline. To do that we need a few things from you by [30 November]. That gives us time to prepare it and for you to check it before it goes in.

1. [item, account, period, acceptable format]
2. [item]
3. [item]

The easiest way to send them is [portal link / receipt app / reply to this email]. Photos and app downloads are fine.

If anything is hard to find, send what you have and tell us what's missing. We can usually work around it. Or book a 15-minute call: [link].

Thanks,
[name]

2. The nudge (day 7)

Subject: Re: What we need for your tax return

Hi [first name],

Just checking this reached you. The list is below again. Anything you can send this week helps.

[numbered list, with anything already received ticked]

Thanks,
[name]

3. The progress update (day 14)

Subject: Thanks, nearly there: [3] things left

Hi [first name],

Thanks for sending [1, 2 and 4]. That's most of it. We still need:

3. [item]
5. [item]
6. [item]

We need these by [30 November] to have your return ready in time. If it's easier to talk it through, I can call you [day/time] — just reply with a time that suits.

Thanks,
[name]

4. The firm reminder (records-due date)

State the consequences as facts with their source. Never "we'll make sure you're fined": HMRC and the IRS charge penalties, not you.

Subject: Your tax return: records needed now to file by 31 January

Hi [first name],

Today was the date we needed your records by, and we still don't have:

3. [item]
6. [item]

Without them we can't finish your return. If it's filed after 31 January, HMRC charges a £100 late filing penalty straight away, even if there's no tax to pay, and more after 3 months. Tax paid late also gets interest, and a 5% penalty after 30 days.

[Only if your engagement letter allows it: As our engagement letter says, records received after [date] are charged at [rate] for the extra work.]

Could you send what you have by [date, about a week away]? If something's stopping you, reply or call me on [number] and we'll find a way.

Thanks,
[name]

US version of the penalty line: "If your return is filed late, the IRS charges 5% of the unpaid tax for each month it's late, up to 25%, and more than 60 days late the minimum is $525 or the full tax due, whichever is less. An extension gives more time to file, but not to pay."

5. Last chance (halfway between records due and the deadline)

Subject: Decision needed on your tax return by [date]

Hi [first name],

The 31 January deadline is [X] days away and we still need [items]. To file on time, we need one of these from you by [date]:

A. Send the missing items by [date], and we'll file on time.
B. [Only if the accountant agrees it's appropriate:] We file using our best estimates for [items] and mark them as provisional. We then amend the return when the final figures arrive, and you'll pay interest on any extra tax from 31 January.
C. We can't file on time. HMRC will charge the £100 late filing penalty, and more the longer it goes on.

Please reply with A, B or C so we have it in writing.

Thanks,
[name]

When something has happened in their life

Hi [first name],

I'm sorry to hear about [what's happened]. Please don't worry about the paperwork right now.

When you're ready, the only things we really need are [the 2 or 3 essentials]. If it helps, we can [get statements with your permission / pick things up / do a short call]. I'll speak to [accountant] about the deadline so you know where you stand.

Take care,
[name]

Then tell the accountant. HMRC and the IRS both accept reasonable excuses such as serious illness or a bereavement, but the return still has to go in once the problem ends.

When the client says "I already sent it"

Hi [first name],

Thanks, and sorry if we've missed it. I've checked [the portal / our inbox] and the last thing we have from you is [item] on [date]. Could you send [items] again, or tell me how you sent them so I can track it down? Uploading to [portal] is the quickest way for it not to get lost.

Thanks,
[name]

Text and WhatsApp versions (under 300 characters)

After two unanswered emails, change channel. Keep it short and point to the full list.

Hi [name], it's [you] from [practice]. We still need 3 things for your tax return: [item], [item], [item]. Full list in your email from [date]. Photos are fine. Can you send them by [date]? Thanks!
Hi [name], [you] here. Your tax return deadline is 31 Jan and we're still missing [item]. Can I call you for 5 minutes this week? Reply with a time that works.

Only text clients who've agreed to be contacted that way, and never send account details or anything sensitive by text.

The penalty facts to quote

Use these as facts with their source, never as threats. They change, so check the current figure before you send.

UK Self AssessmentUS Form 1040
Filing deadline31 January (online) after the tax year ending 5 AprilApril 15. Form 4868 extends filing to October 15, not paying
Late filing£100 at once, even with no tax due. After 3 months, £10 a day for up to 90 days (£900). After 6 months, 5% of the tax due or £300, whichever is more. After 12 months, another 5% or £3005% of the unpaid tax for each month or part month, up to 25%. Over 60 days late: at least $525 (returns due in 2026) or 100% of the tax due, whichever is less
Late payment5% of the tax unpaid at 30 days, 6 months and 12 months, plus interest0.5% of the unpaid tax per month, up to 25%, plus interest
Reasonable excuse / causeYes: appeal the penaltyYes: penalty relief for reasonable cause

Two UK dates clients forget: to pay an underpayment through their tax code, the online return has to be in by 30 December. And if they don't know their final profit, HMRC lets them file with provisional figures, tell HMRC, and amend within 12 months of the deadline, with interest on any extra tax from the original due date.

Sources: Self Assessment penalties, deadlines and sending a return (gov.uk); Failure to file penalty and failure to pay penalty (irs.gov). Read 26 October 2026.

Chasing a whole client list before 31 January

From mid-November, sort every client who isn't finished into three groups, and write one message per group rather than one per client:

GroupWhoWhat to send
GreenEverything in"We have everything, thanks. We'll send your return to approve by [date]."
AmberPartial, but replyingThe progress update (email 3), with their own outstanding items.
RedNothing, or silent after two chasersA text or call, then the firm reminder (email 4). Flag them to the accountant with the last-chance date.

Put the records date in your engagement letter

Chasing is easier when the client agreed the date up front. A scope schedule can say what the client must send, by when, and what happens if it arrives late. An example of the kind of wording practices use (adapt it to your professional body's engagement letter template, and have it checked):

You agree to send us complete records for each tax year by 30 November following the end of that year. If records arrive after that date, we will do our best to file on time, but we cannot guarantee it, and any extra work needed to meet the deadline will be charged at [rate].

Only quote a late-work fee to a client if the letter they signed includes one. For the rest of the letter (scope lines, exclusions and the out-of-scope clause), see our engagement letter template guide and its free schedule builder.

FAQ

What should I do when a client won't send their records?

Send one specific, numbered list of exactly what's missing, with a records-due date and the reason for it. Then chase on a fixed schedule: a nudge after 7 days, a progress update after 14 that thanks them for what arrived, a firm reminder on the records-due date that states the penalties as facts, and a last-chance message with options in writing. Switch to a text or call after two unanswered emails.

When should clients send records for the 31 January deadline?

Work back from 31 January by the time you need to prepare and get the return approved. With 4 to 8 weeks, that's early December to early January. Many practices ask for everything by 30 November. Clients who want an underpayment collected through their tax code need the online return filed by 30 December.

Can I charge a client for sending records late?

Only if your engagement letter says so. Quote the clause in the firm reminder, and keep the tone factual.

Can I file a client's return without all their records?

In the UK, HMRC lets you file with provisional figures if final ones aren't available, tell HMRC they're provisional, and amend within 12 months of the deadline. Interest runs on any extra tax from the original due date. Only do it if the accountant agrees it's appropriate and the client confirms in writing.

A client has stopped replying altogether. When do I give up?

After the last-chance message, write once more confirming what you told them and that you can't file without their records. Keep a dated record of every request. Whether to disengage is a professional decision: follow your professional body's guidance, especially if you suspect anything more than disorganisation.

Is it safe to paste client details into an AI app?

Keep it to what the task needs: a client code or first name, and the list of missing items. Don't paste NI numbers, UTRs, Social Security numbers, full account numbers or ID documents, and check your AI app's data settings and your practice's data-protection policy.
