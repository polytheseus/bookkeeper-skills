Month-end close checklist for bookkeepers: the order that catches mistakes

Most month-end checklists are written for finance teams with a controller and a close calendar. A bookkeeper with twenty small clients needs something lighter that still catches what actually goes wrong in small-business books: an account nobody reconciled, a suspense balance that keeps growing, the same supplier bill entered twice, VAT on the wrong code, and the director's personal shopping booked as stationery. This guide gives the close in five stages, the checks that catch the most errors, and a free builder that dates every task for the month you're closing.

Updated 27 October 2026 · UK and US · Deadlines checked against gov.uk, irs.gov and opm.gov · General information, not accounting or tax advice

In one paragraph: reconcile first, review last. Reconcile every bank, card and payment-processor account to its statement; clear suspense or send one grouped query list; enter sales and bills and check for duplicates; post payroll; check VAT or sales tax; post accruals and prepayments if you're on the accruals basis; update the director's loan or drawings; then review the trial balance for red flags, lock the period and send the client a short summary. Set a target in working days and date each task back from it, so a late bank statement shows up on day 1, not day 5.

The close in five stages

The order matters more than the list. Each stage depends on the one before it: there's no point reviewing a trial balance built on an unreconciled bank account.

The checks that catch the most errors

If you only had time for six checks, these are the ones that find real problems in small-business books:

  1. Statement balance = ledger balance, for every account. Not "the feed is up to date": the closing balance on the bank's statement, written next to the closing balance in the software. Include cards, savings accounts, loans and processor balances (Stripe and PayPal are bank accounts for this purpose). An account that's never been reconciled is where the errors hide.
  2. Suspense is zero, or a short dated list. A suspense or "ask my accountant" balance that grows every month means the questions aren't being asked. Send one grouped list with a reply-by date (our query-list guide has a free builder).
  3. No duplicate bills. Same supplier, same amount, within about a week. Duplicates come from a bill entered from email and again from a receipt app, or a bank-feed rule creating a spend line next to a bill payment.
  4. Payroll and tax liabilities clear. Last month's PAYE, pension or withholding liability should go to zero when it's paid. A balance that grows each month means payments are being coded to expenses, or not being made.
  5. Personal items out. Look at card spending and online shops: personal items go to the director's loan account (companies) or drawings (sole traders), not expenses. Flag an overdrawn director's loan to the accountant; it has tax consequences.
  6. VAT on the right code. Zero-rated, exempt and outside the scope are different codes with different effects on the return. Check reverse charge (including the construction domestic reverse charge), and that no VAT is claimed without a valid VAT invoice.

Cash basis vs accruals basis: what changes at month end

On the accruals basis (traditional accounting), income and costs belong to the month they're earned or incurred. That's why the checklist adds aged debtors and creditors, accruals for bills not yet received, prepayments for costs paid in advance, deferred income and depreciation. UK limited companies use this basis.

On the cash basis, income and costs are recorded when the money moves, so most adjustments drop out. Month end is mostly reconciling, clearing queries, removing personal items and checking dates. In the UK, cash basis is now the starting point for self-employed people's income tax (they can choose traditional accounting instead); limited companies can't use it (gov.uk: cash basis). The client month summary

Clients rarely read a profit and loss report. A short email with the numbers that matter to them, what's coming up and what you need, sent the day you lock the period, is what makes month-end visible (and makes the fee feel worth it). Keep it under 200 words.

Subject: October in numbers, and two quick questions

Hi Sam,

October's books are done. The short version:

Sales £18,400 (September £16,900). Costs £13,100. Profit £5,300.
Bank at 31 October: £11,250. Customers owe you £6,800, of which £2,100 is over 60 days (Ridgeway Homes, £1,450).

Coming up:
- PAYE and NI of about £1,940, due 22 November
- VAT of about £3,600 for the quarter to 31 October, due 7 December

We need from you (by Friday 13 November, please):
1. The £640 payment to "AMZ Mktp" on 14 October: business or personal?
2. A receipt for the £1,200 to Fixings Direct on 21 October.

One thing we noticed: fuel is up 38% on September. Was that the extra Leeds job?

Thanks,
Priya

Example figures. Every number in a real summary should come from the client's books for that month.

Using ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude at month end, safely

  • Good jobs for AI: reviewing a pasted trial balance against last month for red flags, turning uncategorised bank lines into a client query list, and drafting the month summary from figures you give it.
  • Strip identifiers first: use a client code, drop account numbers (or keep the last 4 digits), and never paste NI or Social Security numbers, UTRs or passwords. Check your practice's data-protection policy and the AI app's data settings.
  • Check every figure. AI tools can misread columns or add things up wrong. Every number in a client email should match your software.
  • Keep the decisions. Write-offs, capital vs revenue, director's loan questions and anything about tax treatment go to you and the accountant, not the tool.

FAQ

What is included in a month-end close for a small business?

Reconcile every bank, card and payment-processor account to its statement; clear the suspense account or send the client a query list; enter all sales and bills and check for duplicates; post payroll; check VAT or sales tax; post accruals, prepayments and depreciation if the books are on the accruals basis; update the director's loan account or drawings; review the trial balance for red flags; then lock the period and send the client a short summary.

In what order should I do the month-end close?

Reconcile first, review last. Bank reconciliations come first because every later check depends on the cash being right. Then suspense and missing documents, then sales, purchases and payroll, then adjustments, then the trial balance review, and only then lock the period and report. Reviewing before you've reconciled means reviewing numbers that will change.

How long should month-end take?

For a small client with clean bank feeds, the work itself is often an hour or two; the wait is for the client's records and answers. Set a target in working days that you can hit for every client, put the records-due date in the engagement letter, and date each task backwards from the target. Can I use ChatGPT or another AI tool for month-end?

For reviewing a pasted trial balance or aged report, drafting the client query list and writing the month summary, yes.
