Uncategorised bank transactions: a client query list clients actually answer

Every set of books has them: "FPS J SMITH £1,200", "AMZN MKTP £86.40", a £2,000 transfer in from someone called M Jones. Send the client a 60-line export and nothing comes back. Guess, and personal spending ends up as expenses and loans end up as sales. This guide shows how to sort the lines, what to ask about each kind, a template clients reply to in five minutes, and a free builder that drafts the whole list from your pasted bank lines.

Updated 26 October 2026 · UK and US · Tax points checked against gov.uk and irs.gov · General information, not tax advice

In one paragraph: don't send clients a list of transactions, send them a list of questions. Categorise everything you reasonably can yourself, then ask only what only the client knows. Group the questions by type ("money paid in", "payments to people", "Amazon orders") rather than by date, number every line, give answer options, pre-fill your best guess where you have one ("We think this is stock. Reply OK or correct it"), put the biggest amounts first and stop at 25. Anything that needs professional judgement, like director's loans, capital items, CIS or 1099s, goes to the accountant, not the client.

Sort every line into four groups first

Before you write a single question, go through the lines once and put each into one of these. Most suspense accounts turn out to be half group A.

GroupMeaningExamplesWhat you do
A. ConfidentClear supplier and purposeAdobe, Xero, bank charges, the client's Stripe or SumUp payoutsCategorise it. No question.
B. LikelyProbably X, worth one tap to confirmAmazon, Screwfix, a supermarket, a phone billCategorise it provisionally and ask a yes/no question with your guess filled in.
C. UnknownOnly the client can sayPayments to individuals, cash withdrawals, money in from a personAsk an open question with options.
D. FlagNeeds the accountant's judgement, not just the client's answerDirector's loan items, possible capital purchases, loans, CIS or 1099 questionsA note for the accountant (and usually a client question as well).

Two checks before you go further: the number of lines and the totals in and out should match the export. A line that goes missing here goes missing from the accounts.

What to ask about each kind of transaction

What you seeWhy you can't just guessThe question to ask
Money in that isn't obviously a sale: a transfer from a person, a round amount, a family nameIt could be a sale, a loan, the owner's own money, a refund, a grant or a transfer between their accounts. Booking it as sales overstates profit; booking a sale as a loan hides income."What was this money? Customer payment (which job?) / your own money / a loan / refund / grant / other."
Payments to individuals (FPS, Zelle, Venmo, a name)Wages should go through payroll; a subcontractor may bring CIS (UK) or 1099 (US) duties; it could be a loan repayment or personal."Wages / subcontractor (do you have their invoice?) / loan repayment / refund to a customer / personal / other."
Cash withdrawalsBusiness cost, casual wages or personal. For a sole trader with no evidence, the usual default is drawings; for a company, cash with no receipt usually ends up on the director's loan account."What was this cash used for? Business cost (receipt?) / wages / personal."
Amazon, eBay, PayPal purchasesThe description says where, not what.Batch them: "These 9 Amazon orders look like business supplies. Reply OK, or give the number of any that weren't."
Card-processor and marketplace payouts in (Stripe, SumUp, Square, PayPal, Etsy, eBay)Payouts are net of fees, so the payout isn't the sales figure.Usually no question: record gross sales and the fees separately from the platform's report.
Supermarkets, restaurants, fuelMixed use is normal."Business or personal?" with "personal" pre-filled, or "what share was business?"
Phone, broadband, insuranceOften mixed use, or a personal policy paid from the business account."Business only, mixed (about what %), or personal?"
Large one-off purchasesCould be a capital asset (a van, a laptop, machinery) rather than an expense."What was this? Please send the invoice." Then flag it for the accountant.
Tax payments (HMRC, IRS, state)VAT, payroll, Corporation Tax and personal tax are booked differently, and personal tax paid from a company account isn't a company expense."Which tax was this?" with the options.
Transfers between the client's own accountsNot income or expense, and easy to count twice."Is this a move to your own account? Which one?" Then match both sides.
Refunds and chargebacksNeed linking to the original sale or purchase."Which order was this refund for?"

The client message: a template to copy

This is the format the builder produces. What makes it work: one reason up front, a reply-by date, numbered lines, options to pick from, guesses to confirm, and the biggest amounts first.

Subject: [4] quick questions about your bank statement (reply by [Friday 6 November])

Hi [first name],

We're doing your bookkeeping and there are 4 lines on your bank statement we can't work out on our own. Getting these right means your accounts and tax are correct, and that you don't pay more tax than you need to.

Could you reply by [Friday 6 November]? Just reply with the numbers, e.g. "1 OK, 2 personal, 3 van insurance". If you have a receipt or invoice for anything over £1,000, please send that too.

MONEY PAID IN
1. 08/09  £2,000.00 in  TFR FROM M JONES

   We think this is personal. Reply OK or correct it.

The rules behind it

  • Group by question type, not by date. A client can answer "payments to people" from memory; a date-ordered list makes them think about every line from scratch.
  • Batch similar lines. Nine Amazon orders are one question, not nine.
  • 25 questions at most. Send the smaller ones next week. A long list gets parked "for the weekend" and never answered.
  • Ask for evidence only where it matters: big amounts, possible capital items, and anything where VAT is being reclaimed.
  • Never put your suspicions or the accountant's notes in the client message. Ask neutral, factual questions.
  • No full account or card numbers, in either direction. Last 4 digits are enough.

Notes for the accountant: what not to decide yourself

These need professional judgement. List each with the amount and the facts, keep it neutral, and let the accountant decide.

ItemWhy it matters
Personal costs or tax paid from a company account (UK)They go to the director's loan account, not expenses. If the director owes the company money at the year end and doesn't repay it within 9 months, the company pays a temporary Corporation Tax charge of 33.75% on what's outstanding (reclaimable after repayment), and loans over £10,000 can also be a benefit in kind.
Payments to subcontractors for construction work (UK)Under CIS the contractor verifies the subcontractor with HMRC and deducts 20% (registered), 30% (unregistered) or 0% (gross payment status), and files a monthly return by the 19th.
Payments to contractors (US)Get a W-9 before paying. For payments made in 2026, non-employee compensation of $2,000 or more goes on a 1099-NEC (it was $600 for 2025 payments), and the threshold may be adjusted for inflation from 2027.
Possible capital itemsAnything above the practice's capitalisation threshold may be an asset with capital allowances or depreciation rather than an expense.
Loans and financeLoan receipts are liabilities, not income; repayments need splitting into capital and interest, and hire purchase or lease agreements need the paperwork.
Payments to family membersAre they on payroll and genuinely doing the work?
VAT reclaims (UK)VAT can only be reclaimed with a valid VAT invoice (a simplified invoice is allowed for supplies of £250 or less) and only on the business share of mixed-use costs. No VAT on business entertainment.
Anything unusual for the businessLarge cash deposits, round-sum transfers from unknown sources, payments to unrelated third parties. Write "this needs review", not a theory. If it raises a money-laundering concern, it goes to the practice's nominated officer, and the client must not be told: in the UK, tipping off is an offence.

Sources: Director's loans, What you must do as a CIS contractor and VAT Notice 700 section 16 (gov.uk); About Form 1099-NEC and the 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC instructions (irs.gov). Read 26 October 2026.

Turn the answers into bank rules

Once a client confirms a recurring line, set a rule so you never ask again: "description contains SCREWFIX → Materials, 20% VAT", "ADOBE → Software". Two cautions:

  • No rules for mixed-use suppliers. A rule that sends every Amazon or Tesco line to "Office costs" will be wrong sooner or later, and nobody will notice.
  • No rules for payments to people. The same name can be wages one month and a loan repayment the next.

A monthly routine that keeps suspense near zero

  1. Week 1: reconcile, apply bank rules, and sort what's left into groups A–D.
  2. Send the query list (25 questions at most, biggest first) with a reply-by date about a week out.
  3. Week 2: post the answers, add rules for anything recurring, and send the accountant the group D notes.
  4. If there's no reply, one nudge with the same list, then a text. If a client is regularly silent, our records chasing guide has the full chasing sequence.
  5. Before the year end, anything still in suspense goes on a single list for the accountant with the amount and what you've already asked.

Monthly lists of 5 to 10 questions get answered. A year's worth in January doesn't, and with Making Tax Digital for Income Tax in place since April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000, quarterly updates make "sort it out at the year end" much harder anyway.

FAQ

How do I get clients to answer questions about their bank transactions?

Send one short list, grouped by type (money paid in, payments to people, Amazon orders) rather than by date, with every line numbered and answer options to pick from. Pre-fill your best guess where you have one ("We think this is stock. Reply OK or correct it"), put the biggest amounts first, keep it to 25 questions or fewer, and give a reply-by date. Clients can then answer "1 OK, 2 personal, 3 van insurance" from their phone.

What is a suspense account in bookkeeping?

A temporary holding account for transactions you can't yet categorise. It should be cleared, not lived in: every line in it needs either a client answer or an accountant's decision before the accounts are finalised. Clearing it monthly with a short query list is far easier than facing a year of unknowns in January.

Should money paid into a client's account be treated as sales?

Not until you know what it is. Money in can be a sale, a loan, the owner's own money, a refund, a grant or a transfer from another of their accounts. Booking it all as sales overstates profit and tax; booking a sale as a loan understates it. Ask, and keep the answer.

What happens if a director pays personal tax from the company account?

It isn't a company expense. It goes to the director's loan account. If the director owes the company money at the year end and it isn't repaid within 9 months, the company pays a temporary Corporation Tax charge of 33.75% of the amount outstanding (reclaimable once repaid), and loans over £10,000 can also be a taxable benefit. Flag it for the accountant.

Do I need a 1099-NEC for a contractor in 2026?

For payments made in 2026, the IRS threshold for reporting non-employee compensation rose to $2,000 (it was $600 for 2025 payments) and may be adjusted for inflation from 2027. Get a W-9 from every contractor before you pay them, so you have what you need either way.

Can I paste a client's bank transactions into ChatGPT?

Only what the task needs. Strip account and card numbers (the builder on this page shows only the last 4 digits), use a client code instead of a name, and check your AI app's data settings and your practice's data-protection policy before pasting anything.
