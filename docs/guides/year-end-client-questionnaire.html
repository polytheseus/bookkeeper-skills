Year-end client questionnaire: what to ask before the tax return

The generic year-end questionnaire is twelve pages long and comes back half-filled. A good one asks each client only what applies to them, says why in one line, and catches what people always forget: the side income, the pension top-up, the shares they sold, the baby. This guide has the questions for each kind of client (UK Self Assessment and US Form 1040), the document checklist, a cover email, and a free builder that writes the pack with a records-due date.

Updated 26 October 2026 · UK and US · Rules checked against gov.uk and irs.gov · General information, not tax advice

In one paragraph: send each client a short numbered list of yes/no questions that apply to them, each with a one-line "why we ask", grouped as: everyone (other income, pensions, charity, asset sales, family changes), their client type (sole trader, landlord, director; or Schedule C, rental, K-1), and anything that changed this year. Put a records-due date at the top, worked back from the filing deadline by your prep time, and end with a checklist of what to send for each "yes". Aim for 15 to 25 questions for a simple client.

What makes a year-end questionnaire actually come back

  • Only what applies. A landlord with no business doesn't need eight questions about stock and mileage. Every irrelevant question makes the relevant ones easier to skip.
  • Yes/no first, detail only on a yes. "Did you pay into a personal pension yourself?" gets answered. "Please provide details of all pension arrangements" doesn't.
  • One line of "why we ask". Clients answer questions they understand. "HMRC already gets figures from your bank" explains why the savings account matters.
  • Number everything, so replies can say "yes to 4 and 9, attached".
  • A date with a reason. "By Friday 4 December, so we can prepare it and check it with you before Christmas" beats "as soon as possible".
  • Say what good enough looks like. A photo, an app download, a screenshot of the HMRC account.
  • Don't collect sensitive numbers by email. Questions by email are fine; NI or Social Security numbers, full account numbers, passwords and ID go through a portal or the phone.

The things clients forget (ask every time)

  1. The second income. A small rental, a side business, online selling, a few hundred pounds or dollars of savings interest.
  2. The pension top-up paid from their own bank account, not through payroll.
  3. The sale. Shares from an old employer scheme, crypto swapped for other crypto, a buy-to-let sold in the summer.
  4. The family change. A move, a marriage or separation, a baby (UK Child Benefit charge; US dependants and credits).
  5. The vehicle bought on finance, and the finance agreement that goes with it.
  6. The letter. An HMRC or IRS notice that arrived in May and has been on the fridge since.
  7. US 2025 onwards: tips, overtime and new car loan interest, which only show up if you ask.

The cover email

Short, with the date and the reason, how long it takes, and an offer of a call. The builder fills in the client's numbers; this is the shape:

Subject: Your tax return: 17 questions and a records date (Friday 4 December 2026)

Hi Sam,

It's time to get your return ready. The filing deadline is Sunday 31 January 2027, and so we have time to prepare it, check it with you and file it calmly, please send everything by Friday 4 December 2026.

The questionnaire below only has questions that apply to you. Most are yes or no, and each says why we ask. If the answer is yes, the list at the end says what to send. It should take about 30 minutes.

Photos and app downloads are fine. Please don't email passwords, full account numbers or ID documents: send those through our secure portal or tell us by phone.

If something's changed or you're not sure what applies, reply and we'll book a quick call.

Thanks,
Priya

Text version, for clients who don't open email:

Hi Sam, it's Priya. Your tax return questions are in your inbox: 17 quick ones, about 30 minutes. Could you send them back with the documents by Fri 4 Dec? Reply here if you'd rather do it on a call.

Your review checklist when it comes back

  • Every income source from last year is here this year, or the client has said why not.
  • Turnover roughly agrees with bank receipts, platform reports (HMRC digital platform data, 1099-K) and any VAT returns.
  • Capital items are separated from everyday costs; personal and mixed-use items are adjusted.
  • Payments on account or estimated payments agree with the HMRC or IRS account.
  • Losses brought forward and capital allowance pools or depreciation are carried over.
  • Any HMRC or IRS letters have been dealt with.
  • The client has approved the return in writing before you file, and you've kept the approval.

FAQ

What should a year-end client questionnaire include?

Only the questions that apply to that client: everyone gets income they might have missed, pensions, charity, asset sales and family changes; then add sections for their type and this year's events. Make them yes/no first with a one-line reason, number them, and end with a checklist of documents for each yes and a records-due date.

When should clients send their records for the 31 January deadline?

Work back from the deadline by the time you need to prepare the return and get it approved. With 6 to 8 weeks, that's early to mid December. Many practices ask for everything by 30 November, and clients who want an underpayment collected through their tax code need the online return filed by 30 December.

What do clients most often forget?

A second income source, personal pension contributions and Gift Aid, sales of shares, crypto or a second property, Child Benefit when income is over £60,000, and life changes such as moving, marrying or having a child. In the US, add late 1099s, estimated payments, Marketplace cover and, from 2025, tips, overtime and new car loan interest.

What are the new US deductions for 2025 to 2028?

An extra $6,000 deduction for people 65 and over, up to $25,000 of qualified tips, up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint) of qualified overtime, and up to $10,000 of interest on a loan for a new, US-assembled personal vehicle. They're claimed on Schedule 1-A whether or not the client itemizes and phase out at higher incomes. Check the eligibility rules on irs.gov.

Should I send the same questionnaire to every client?

No. Keep one master list and delete what doesn't apply per client. A tailored 15-question list gets answered; a generic 60-question one gets half-filled and returned in February.

Is it safe to send a questionnaire by email?

The questions are fine by email. Ask clients not to email passwords, full account numbers, National Insurance or Social Security numbers or ID documents; collect those through a secure portal or by phone. If you use an AI app to draft packs, give it a first name or client code and the facts, not identifiers.
